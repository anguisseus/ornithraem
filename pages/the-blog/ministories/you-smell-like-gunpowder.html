You smell like gunpowder.

October 24th, 2025

The streets were dark and slick. A man ran through the waterlogged roads, the rain striking his face as he darted forward. He was late. His briefcase slipped from his hand just as a gunshot echoed through the alley to his left. He jumped.

A dark figure bolted from the scene, the sound of metal clattering against the pavement following close behind.

The man hesitated, then crept toward the noise. His steps trembled as he drew closer. A crimson liquid flowed past his shoes, swirling into the runoff. Water dripped from his face as he crouched beside the dark shape on the ground. It was a person.

He fell back, pressing himself against the cold wall of the alley.

Police sirens filled the night, red and blue flashes flickering across the soaked neighborhood. The sound faded as a patrol car came to a halt. Two officers stepped out, spotting the lone briefcase before sweeping their flashlights down the alley.

They froze at the sight — a man sitting beside a corpse, a gun gleaming beside him.

A silent dread hung heavy.

“What the—” one of the officers finally broke the stillness.

“Dispatch, this is 7-Adam-19. I need immediate backup at 1420 East Hill Street. We’ve got one male at gunpoint, one victim down, firearm still hot. Requesting additional units and paramedics, Code 3,” the other reported, voice tight.

They moved cautiously forward. One officer used a napkin to lift the gun and set it aside. The other knelt, pressing two fingers to the victim’s neck. No pulse.

The radio crackled, “7-Adam-19, confirm one DOA. Cancel paramedics. Continue CSI and coroner.”

Five minutes later, the CSI team arrived — and with them, a detective, waiting for answers.

“The suspect’s unresponsive, and the victim was dead when we got here,” one officer explained. “We’re taking him to the station.”

“Put him in for questioning. I’ll be there once I’m done here.” The detective slid on his gloves. “Take the gun and briefcase to Evidence”

“Right away, detective.”

“Come on, put him in the back,” the other officer said, cuffing the man.

“Hey detective, I know it isn’t my job to butt in,” the officer added quietly, “but you smell like gunpowder.”

“Yeah, I was at the range earlier. Didn’t bother changing.” He tugged at his tie nervously. “Thanks for bringing it up.”

“Yeah, no problem. Just change before the captain notices that smell.”

He finished cuffing the man and shoved him into the back of the cruiser. Sirens wailed once again through the night as the man sat in stunned silence, slowly coming to terms with his fate.
