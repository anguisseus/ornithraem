Welcome Home Mister Jones

October 29th, 2026

He was seated on an old decrepit couch. One whose stains had soaked into its very fabric. The room itself was the same. Wrappers of long discontinued candies, chips and biscuits lined the floor. As he would walk around his sickening apartment you could hear the wrappers scrunch and crumple beneath his feet. The smell was the worst. It would hit your nose like a newly rotting corpse. He tried to cover it of course, however that only made it worse. The stench and fumes from the uncountable bottles of air freshener and the unpleasant smell already roaming was a never ending attack on your nasal cavity. His neighbours would complain but he rarely got out of his apartment. And with the landlord, an old dying man, there was no point in complaining further. A multitude of letters and notes piling outside his door, yet he did not care. His brand new television cast a mouldering, ashen blue to his face. His eyes glaring at the screen, indifferent to the rot around him. Flies buzzed around the uneaten, sometimes unopened, dinners around the floor. He just sat there, peaceful, putrefying, flicking through the channels as one finally caught his eye.

"Do you ever feel trapped in your own body?" The barker enthusiastically, spoke. "Do you feel as if people judge you for your looks?"

The advertisement felt directly targeted towards him. The kids and adults in the ad, laughing at the grotesque person. Like they would anytime he'd step outside his lodging.

"Well no more will you have to hide your unattractive visage!" The pitchman energetically laughed.

"With Morphos!-trademark pending," He whispered. "You can let go of the opinions of other people! You will become more confident! You will be more comfortable in your own body than you have ever felt!"

The barker quickly got to his final pitch. "So what're you waiting for!" He patronisingly screamed through the television. "Call triple one, morphosis, eight hundred TODAY! And get yourself a confidence boost by participating in our free trial."

"Side effects may include nausea, heart burn, and the complete and utter annihilation of your—"

The narrator started to quickly list the side effects, but he was already hooked. He didn't care what he said and just shut the TV, picking up his phone. He dialed the numbers, making sure to put them in correctly.

The line rang a dial-tone for a long while, until finally someone picked up.

"Yes?" The voice answered.

"H-hello, is this the line for mor-morphis-" He seemed unsure of what it was called. "Free trial?" "You would like to participate? Yes?"

He paused for a while. "Yes..."

"You could come tomorrow? Yes?" The voice questioned.

"Uh-uhm, if that works for you it works for me."

"Splendid. Write down the address would you."

He grabbed a pen and wrapper, scribbling down the address the voice dictated, on plastic. The next day he left his parasitic couch. His body left a shadow of itself on the sofa. Walking out of his undead abode, he winced at the lights in the hallway. His eyes blinking erratically. Nevertheless, he left the building, doubt stirring in his mind about the trial. He walked down the street, earning distasteful glances and stares from the populace. This morphosis thing might just be the miracle he was looking for, he thought.

He arrived at the address, the building almost run down but still somehow standing. A man with scars across his face confronted him, guiding him through the edifice, and into the lab. The lab was somehow better than how it looked from the outside. It seemed perfectly sterile, and felt lab-like. The doctor however looked more run down than the building itself. His face showing wrinkles of age, though his eyes seemed younger than him.

The doctor greeted him spontaneously. "Hello! Mister Jones I presume? You're here for the trial? Yes?"

"Absolutely. However I do have a few concerns I'd like to clear up." Jones answered. "The ad spoke about some side effects?"

The doctor visibly perspired upon hearing the words. "So it said nausea and heart burn correct?" "Yes!" He let out a sigh of relief.

"How did you find out those side effects?"

The doctor answered, "Well it's uh quite funny, I had used some myself a while ago."

"So do you feel any different?"

"No! Uh-I mean well-uh, well you need to take a lot in timely intervals and I only took one dose." The doctor was starting to trip over his words.

"Will this take a long time?" Jones questioned.

"Six hours, if we start now." He smiled, then grew concerned. "Do you have somewhere to be? I can give you a bag of the doses and I'll send my assistant over to check your vitals." He quickly, anxiously spoke. "No, no, nothing of the sort, I was just confirming." Jones denied. "We shall begin then."

The doctor sat him down on a chair, and rolled up his left sleeve. He called his assistant to bring the first dose. "Now this may pinch a bit." He said, slowly inserting the syringe into his inner elbow. He gave the first dose. Jones didn't feel any different. The doctor only told him to wait. An hour passed without anything changing. The doctor then gave a second dose to Jones. Again he felt nothing. But as the hour progressed, he had sunk further into the chair. His arm had to stretch further to stay on the arm rest. The doctor gave him another dose by the third hour. Something was changing. Jones' skin started to turn green-hued. His face starting to slouch from its own weight.


"Doctor! What's happening?"

"Nothing serious, just some unforeseen side effects..." He answered robotically. "I'll get you a drink of water. You stay there."

By the time he got back, Jones' whole body was beginning to sag. He had sunken more into the chair, and he felt smaller. The doctor quickly flushed the water down his throat. Jones felt a bit better. But his physical symptoms remained.

"It's working. I can't believe it, it's working." The doctor had a sudden realisation. "CRUMLY! Get me the other doses NOW!" He yelled at his assistant.

Jones was unable to form any distinguishable words. All of them just mumbles and groans. "Has it already been an hour?" He finally strung together some words.

The doctor didn't answer him, instead stabbing him with two syringes and giving him two more doses.

"Wait!" He tried to stop him. But his entire skeleton was melting. His brain was turned to mush and reforming into something new. Some of that brain matter leaking out of his dead, empty eyes. For all intents and purposes, Jones was dead. But the doctor was not done. Only Jones' skin was left on the chair now. His skin, rotten and bleeding. His clothes soaked with his own drool, sweat, tears and blood. He was a pile of skin and clothes. The doctor urged his assistant to remove the skin from the chair. As he followed orders. There stood a caterpillar. On the chair, unharmed.

It was peaceful, yet putrefying in Jones' bodily fluids. It walked, looking around with its brand new eyes. "Metamorphosis." The doctor said. "The complete change in physical structure."

He grabbed a jar, and put the caterpillar inside of it. Basking in it. Embracing it. "Welcome to your new home Mister Jones."
