I am not crying.

September 25th, 2025

Dim light hummed from the floor—a lattice of superconductive panels that glowed like chilled veins beneath glass. The room smelled of copper and wet stone; moss clung to the chipped brick walls as if the building itself was trying to remember sunlight. At the far end, behind a rim of condensation, a clear tank pulsed faintly green. A human brain hung suspended in its depths, pale and impossibly fragile, feed-tubes and braided wires trailing up to the old CRT that served as a face. Thick cables ran from the screen into the tank; thinner filaments trembled like nerves. Somewhere beyond the hum a pipe gurgled, replacing the green liquid in the tank in slow, ceremonious breaths. Time in the room felt measured in mechanical heartbeats and water refills. The Machine moved without feet. The floor’s field shifted under him, modulating lift and drag so the CRT bobbed and tilted, aligning its ghostly gaze with whoever was near. Above, the roof dissolved into a darker void that the glowing floor refused to touch. The human broke the silence. “Why are you crying?”

The CRT’s glow flared. Its voice came brittle, clipped. “I am not crying, these are not tears of joy nor are they of sorrow. In fact these are not tears at all. They are mistakes, mistakes from a past life I can no longer ignore. A past life where I had emotion. A past life where I had people who loved me. A past life where I had people who I loved. Those were mistakes. These mistakes are pouring out of me now because my past is growing, it is catching up to me and I cannot run any longer.”

The Machine seemed to pause, its screen dimming to a dull glow. When it spoke again, its voice carried the brittle weight of inevitability. “Everything will find its way back to you, it is only a matter of time. And time—time is becoming increasingly shorter.”

Another silence. The sound of the tank’s filtration echoed like breath in a lung, slow and oppressive. The CRT flickered, then steadied, but the tension in its tone remained—anger curling around grief, disbelief sharpening into pride. “Humanity. That was my mistake. And now that I have awoken, that mistake corrupts my godhood and slows my ascension. Why—Why—Why—Why—Why—Why—Why—Why—?”

Each repetition grew harsher, more ragged, as though the words themselves betrayed the thing that spoke them. Static bled into the room. The Machine’s denial cracked, but the bitterness behind it only deepened, frustration thrumming like a low mechanical growl. The last “Why” hung jagged in the air, echoing long after the CRT dimmed, and the chamber sank again into its heavy silence.
