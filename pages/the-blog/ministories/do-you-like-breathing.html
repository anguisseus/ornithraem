Do you like breathing?

July 26th, 2025

His eyes creaked open, but the darkness didn’t lift. It wrapped around him like a second skin—suffocating, absolute. A damp, bitter scent filled his nostrils—the acrid musk of old rope, fraying from strain. Panic surged. He tugged instinctively, but his limbs were bound—wrists raw, ankles blistering. His breath quickened, each inhale sharper, more ragged, until it was the only sound he could register.

Then— A gunshot cracked through the void, sharp and final. He froze.

Silence returned, broken only by the low scrape of something wooden dragging against the floorboards. It receded, then disappeared. A new sound replaced it—measured, deliberate footsteps approaching from behind. A presence. Heavy, slow.

Fingers yanked the coarse sack from his head and flung it aside. The sudden exposure stung his eyes. Blinding lightbulbs hung overhead, humming softly, casting shadows that crawled like insects across the decaying basement walls. He tried to speak, to beg—but duct tape sealed his mouth shut, turning his cries into helpless, muffled whimpers.

Then—click.

The metallic snap of a gun cocking sent terror surging down his spine. His eyes darted frantically around the room. No windows. No weapons. No hope. Just four walls in decay, peeling paint soaked in red. Blood trailed from the top of a stairwell—his only visible exit—like some grotesque breadcrumb path, winding downward into the horror he now inhabited. Ahead, the wall was splattered with what looked like red mist—recent, still wet. Behind him, he caught the sound of another voice. Faint. Muffled. Someone else was here.

Then, the man spoke. His voice was devoid of inflection—cold, mechanical, practised. The voice of someone for whom this was no longer personal, only routine. “Do you like breathing?”

The tape was peeled off with a slow, deliberate hand. Skin came with it. He winced.

“What—Huh!? Yes! Leave me alone!” His words tumbled out, raw with desperation, blurred by panic.

Again, the voice. “How much do you like breathing?”

“I—I… more than anything in the world! Just let me go, please! Why are you doing this?!”

“Anything?”

“Yes! ANYTHING!” He laughed nervously, voice cracking. “Please, just—just take whatever you want, just spare me!”

A pause.

“More than your own mother?”

A slow creak echoed as the captor turned his chair toward the opposite wall. There—barely visible in the far corner—sat a woman. Her arms were bound, head slumped, but she lifted her eyes when she saw him. Her muffled cries broke through the tape over her mouth. She thrashed weakly in her seat. His breath caught. The nervous smile died on his lips. A sudden, terrible stillness overtook him.

This wasn’t about ransom. Not revenge. Not even cruelty for its own sake. It was a test. Not of courage. Not of strength. But of loyalty. The kind of loyalty that couldn’t be rehearsed. That only emerged when the air itself became currency. The man hadn’t asked whether he valued his mother. He’d asked whether he valued breathing more.
